After operational planning · Consider financial risk

Reduce waste first, then consider sharing losses

Adjusting seats, shifts and supplies comes first. A weather-sensitive business could also consider hedging: a contract that pays under an adverse weather outcome may offset part of a business loss. This differs from arbitrage and does not guarantee a profit.

How does an ice cream shop respond to cold weather?

The New York Post reported on Los Angeles ice cream shop 28wishes on July 16, 2026. Owner Jason Jiang said business fell about 20% below 70°F (about 21°C). The brothers put about $20 a day into Kalshi weather markets, seeking to offset business losses with cold-weather contract returns.

He reported monthly earnings of up to $1,500.

(The report uses Kalshi, not Polymarket. Kalshi is also a prediction market where participants can trade contracts on future event outcomes.)

Read the original · New York Post, July 16, 2026 → Read the same-author republication →

The report uses Kalshi to illustrate prediction-market hedging. A comparable Polymarket approach requires matching place, date and risk, and assessment of prices, fees and tradability. Contracts can lose money and may not sufficiently offset business losses.

Mexico City · Café Patio · Fictional scenario

Will tomorrow's customers sit outside or move indoors?

Diego runs a café called Café Patio in Mexico City. Before closing, he still needs to settle tomorrow’s plans: should all outdoor tables be set up? Does the morning shift need another person, or would help be more useful in the afternoon? How much stock should he order for cold drinks without leaving too much unused?

Rain could leave outdoor seats unused and bring customers inside. On a hot, sunny day, shaded tables and cold drinks might be more popular. With weather expectations still divided, the owner wants to keep seating, shifts, and supplies flexible.

Diego wants to know: what weather is most likely tomorrow? Where were people active on similar days? Which preparations can be made tonight, and which can wait?

03

From preparation to managing business risk

Understand changing weather and help a small business plan ahead

Returning to Diego’s Café Patio in Mexico City: our visualization could help Diego read likely temperature ranges, see how concentrated expectations are, and compare past forecasts with observations, then use official forecasts to plan seating, staffing, and supplies.

Colder weather

If expectations point to cooler weather, the owner could prepare indoor seating, adjust hot and cold drink supplies, and keep staffing flexible as outdoor foot traffic changes.

Warmer weather

If expectations point to warmer weather, the owner could check shade and ventilation, prepare cold drinks, and use past foot-traffic records to plan outdoor seating and staffing.

When expectations are spread out, the owner could prepare supplies in stages and leave room to adjust. The tool would not make decisions for the owner; it would make weather information easier to read and support preparation before conditions change.

The café is a simulated case, and charted activity is not revenue. Practical use requires market data for the relevant place and date, alongside official forecasts and the café’s own business records.

Wider significance · Climate adaptation and sustainable development

From understanding information to climate adaptation

1. Information equity

We want more people to understand weather records and market prices and use them to plan everyday decisions.

Expand the explanation

Weather information is scattered across sources and often uses specialist terms. We bring the records together and explain how to read and compare them.

Official observations, Polymarket quotes and historical outcomes are publicly available. Reading them takes some explanation: which range has the highest quote, how spread out prices are, and how past judgments compare with recorded weather.

The charts put weather records, market quotes and past outcomes together, with explanations of the trading mechanism. Readers can learn to read the charts, then consider how the information might inform travel, drying food or running a business. We hope this makes the material easier to understand across different levels of specialist knowledge.

Information equity means helping people access data, understand it and make their own choices.

2. Climate adaptation and sustainable development

SDG 13.3 · Understand risk information

Explaining weather uncertainty through charts is one way this project aims to build awareness of climate risk.

Expand the explanation

SDG 13.3 addresses climate education and awareness. This project aims to help ordinary readers understand weather-risk information.

Weather information includes temperatures, probabilities, warnings, market quotes and historical records. Comparing NOAA temperatures with Polymarket range quotes helps readers inspect favored outcomes, price concentration and differences from recorded weather.

Plain explanations and comparable charts help readers understand weather uncertainty, discuss risk and plan their next steps.

SDG 13.1 · Prepare for changing conditions

Adjusting everyday plans in advance can help people respond to changing weather. Its value needs evaluation in local use.

Expand the explanation

For SDG 13.1, the project focuses on how readers can prepare after learning about weather risk.

Planning weather-dependent activities requires a range of possible conditions and an understanding of uncertainty. Outdoor travel, food drying, waterside tourism and small businesses may adjust their timing, scale or preparation as weather conditions change.

Comparing market signals, quote distributions and historical records helps readers adjust plans in advance. Start with local official forecasts and warnings, then consider market information alongside your own circumstances.
