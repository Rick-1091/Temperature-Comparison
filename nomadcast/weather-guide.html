Make weather expectations easier to understand

(1) Understand the weather expectation

An expected temperature range, the spread of expectations, and past comparisons can inform travel, drying food, and household plans. Reading this information does not require buying contracts.

(2) Turn expectations into everyday preparation

For this household, useful information means preparing earlier: when rain becomes more likely, arrange covers and space to move grain; when expectations remain divided, dry in batches rather than exposing the whole harvest at once.

They do not need trading terminology or contract purchases. A simple view should show possible weather, how concentrated expectations are, and how past expectations compared with reality. It supports the next step without making the decision for them.

(3) When usual weather-information channels are disrupted

In a future crisis, local television or radio weather information may be disrupted. If internet access remains available and Polymarket has a weather market for the relevant location and date, our visualization could offer another information channel by showing market expectations.

Users could read the most likely temperature range, compare outcome probabilities and historical results, and keep travel, drying, or business plans adaptable. For dangerous weather, local meteorological and emergency warnings should remain the priority.

Our New York temperature comparison shows how to read predictions; it is not a rainfall forecast for Rumphi.
