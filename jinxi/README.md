# Jinxi Weather Intro

Opening page for the team’s weather, data, and adaptation project.

## Role in the full website

This page is a field-based entry point, not a claim that Jinxi residents use prediction markets or need a particular weather tool. It establishes the project’s central framing:

`weather conditions → place-based activity → local decisions → data signals`

The final section hands users to the team’s later pages on data infrastructure, prediction signals/AI, and comparative adaptation.

## Evidence boundary and SDG claim

The page documents observed settings and museum displays from a course visit. It does **not** establish current weather-related losses, current business practices, demand for the project, or local use of prediction platforms. It also does not treat museum interpretation as the complete account of Jinxi.

The relevant target is **SDG 13.3**: “Improve education, awareness-raising and human and institutional capacity on climate change mitigation, adaptation, impact reduction and early warning.” This classroom page makes only a limited contribution to that target: it can support students’ and visitors’ awareness of how weather uncertainty may relate to place-based activity and how different information sources can be compared. It does not demonstrate increased local capacity, improved adaptation outcomes, or a benefit to Jinxi residents.

## Local review and correction route

We invite a Jinxi-based reviewer—such as staff from either named museum, a local heritage worker, or a tourism/shop operator—to review the captions and the page’s interpretation of place-based activity. A reviewer may identify an incorrect caption, missing context, or an inappropriate image/claim.

For this course project, corrections or removal requests can be submitted in the course-platform discussion attached to the team submission, with the image filename or section heading and the requested change. The team will acknowledge the request within seven days, correct or remove the relevant material where warranted, and record the change in this README before any public reuse. The page must not be presented as locally validated unless a reviewer’s feedback and consent are recorded here.
