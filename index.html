Africa · Malawi · A simulated household in a documented setting

Thoko: keep drying the grain, or bring it in?

Thoko lives with her family in Rumphi, northern Malawi. Their small plot provides food, and they sell some produce to support daily life. After harvest, Thoko and her family spread maize out to dry, hoping to use the sunshine before storing it.

They are not entirely without forecasts: radio carries weather reports, and a phone brings occasional messages. But a broad forecast is hard to apply to their village, and scattered messages do not explain when rain might arrive or how certain the expectation is. What they lack is timely, locally relevant information they can understand.

Keep drying, and unexpected rain could wet the grain and require more work. Bring it in early, and they may miss a useful dry spell. With limited storage and income, either choice affects labour, food, and household spending. They must decide without a clear picture of tomorrow's weather.

Their questions are simple: what is tomorrow likely to bring, how certain is that expectation, and what should we prepare for?

Documented context: access and understanding barriers

UNDP's Malawi project documents technical, financial, and weather-information access barriers, and develops tailored forecasts and agricultural advisories for food-insecure districts. Related research also identifies literacy and interpretation barriers.
